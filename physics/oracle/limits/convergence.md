# Discrete pendulum against the hanging rope: convergence

Generated by `convergence.py` (run it from `physics/oracle`: `uv run --no-sync python limits/convergence.py`). Do not edit by hand.

Chain: N equal masses M/N on rods of length L/N (the paper's section-5 system), linearized from the Lagrangian, solved as the generalized symmetric eigenproblem K v = omega^2 M v (`scipy.linalg.eigh`). Rope: omega_k = (j_{0,k}/2) sqrt(g/L), Psi_k(y) = J0(j_{0,k} sqrt(1 - y/L)) (`rope.py`). N doubles as in the paper, n_p = n 2^p, from n = 1 to N = 1024. g = 9.81, L = 2.0, M = 3.0; every number below is a dimensionless ratio, so the values of g, L, M do not matter.

Errors: frequency, omega_k(N)/omega_k - 1; shape, max over the masses of |v_j - Psi_k(y_j)| with y_j = jL/N, both normalized to (1/N) sum v_j^2 = (1/L) int Psi^2 dy = 1 and positive at the free end. Order: log2 of the error ratio between N/2 and N.

N*err tends to -1/4 for each fixed mode: omega_k(N) = omega_k (1 - 1/(4N) + O(N^-2)), first order. The modes tabulated here are slower than the rope's. That is not true of every mode at a given N: the O(N^-2) term grows with the square of the mode number, and the modes above about 1.6 sqrt(N) are faster than the rope's.
